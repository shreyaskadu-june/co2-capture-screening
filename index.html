Method, sources and limits

  • Mass balance: CO₂ captured = flue gas (kmol/h) × CO₂ fraction × capture rate. Flue gas molar mass uses 28.5 kg/kmol for the non-CO₂ fraction (dry, bulk N₂/O₂).
  • Solvent circulation: 30 wt% MEA contains 4.91 mol MEA per kg solution. Each mol of CO₂ loaded per mol MEA therefore carries 0.216 kg CO₂ per kg solution. Circulation = CO₂ captured ÷ (0.216 × (rich − lean loading)).
  • Check against the literature: at 5.5% CO₂ and 90% capture, with a loading swing of 0.19–0.23, this gives a solvent-to-gas ratio of about 1.5–1.8 kg/kg. Rezazadeh et al. (2016) report 1.58–1.64 kg/kg at their optimised conditions ✓ [source]
  • Specific regeneration energy: piecewise-linear through three points from Rezazadeh et al. (2016): 4.4 MJ/kg at lean loading 0.21 (IMTP25 packing), 3.64 MJ/kg at 0.26 (Mellapak 250Y), and 7.1 MJ/kg at 0.165 (base configuration, not optimised). The 0.165 point comes from a different configuration and should be treated as indicative.
  • Carbon intensity: gas emissions factor 0.18231 kg CO₂e/kWh (gross CV) and grid electricity 0.13096 kg CO₂e/kWh (location-based, generation only), both from the DESNZ 2026 GHG conversion factors. Net capture ratio = 1 − regeneration CO₂ ÷ CO₂ captured.
  • Not included: column diameter, packing height and flooding; absorber and stripper temperature profiles; solvent degradation and make-up; capital and operating cost; CO₂ compression; cooling duty; water balance; any part of the process other than the amine loop.
  • Validity: results are only as good as the inputs. Rich loading, lean loading and specific energy should come from a validated simulation or pilot data for the actual solvent and packing before any decision.
